Business case: Chinook models a digital media store (an iTunes-like online music shop).
Source: the chinook-database README, not the shipped data.

- The catalogue: a few hundred artists, each with one or more albums; albums contain tracks.
  The track table is the largest catalogue table (thousands of tracks). Each track has a name,
  an album, a media type (a handful of formats such as MPEG audio, AAC, video), a genre (about
  25 genres, rock and latin being the most common), an optional composer, a duration in
  milliseconds (mostly 2-6 minutes), a size in bytes and a unit price (0.99 for songs, 1.99 for
  videos).
- Playlists group tracks; a small number of playlists (about 20), some very large.
- The store has a small staff (fewer than ten employees) organised in a reporting hierarchy:
  each employee except the general manager reports to another employee. Employees have titles,
  hire dates, birth dates and contact details.
- Customers (dozens, international, spread across many countries with the largest share in the
  USA and Canada) each have a support representative who is an employee. Customer records carry
  well-formatted contact data; company is optional (mostly empty).
- Sales: each purchase is an invoice for a customer with an invoice date, billing address and
  total; an invoice has several invoice lines, each for one track at its unit price with a
  quantity (always 1). Sales are spread evenly over a four-year period; invoices number in the
  hundreds, invoice lines in the thousands.

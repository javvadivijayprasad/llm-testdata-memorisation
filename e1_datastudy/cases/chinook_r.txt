Business case: the company runs a digital media store (an online music shop).

- The catalogue: a few hundred performers, each with one or more records; records contain songs.
  The song table is the largest catalogue table (thousands of songs). Each song has a name,
  a record, a media type (a handful of formats such as MPEG audio, AAC, video), a style (about
  25 styles, rock and latin being the most common), an optional composer, a duration in
  milliseconds (mostly 2-6 minutes), a size in bytes and a unit price (0.99 for songs, 1.99 for
  videos).
- Collections group songs; a small number of collections (about 20), some very large.
- The store has a small staff (fewer than ten workers) organised in a reporting hierarchy:
  each worker except the general manager reports to another worker. Workers have titles,
  hire dates, birth dates and contact details.
- Clients (dozens, international, spread across many countries with the largest share in the
  USA and Canada) each have a support representative who is a worker. Client records carry
  well-formatted contact data; company is optional (mostly empty).
- Sales: each purchase is a bill for a client with a bill date, billing address and
  total; a bill has several bill lines, each for one song at its unit price with a
  quantity (always 1). Sales are spread evenly over a four-year period; bills number in the
  hundreds, bill lines in the thousands.

Business case: BikeStores is a bicycle retailer with three shops in the north-eastern United
States, selling bikes from a number of brands and keeping per-store stock.
Source: the BikeStores sample-database description on sqlservertutorial.net, not the shipped data.

- Production side. Brands: about nine bicycle brands. Categories: about seven product categories
  (children's bicycles, comfort bicycles, cruisers, cyclocross bicycles, electric bikes, mountain
  bikes, road bikes). Products: a few hundred bicycle models, each with a name that includes the
  brand and model year, a brand, a category, a model year (over a four-year span) and a list price
  from a few hundred to several thousand dollars. Stocks: for each store and product, the quantity
  on hand (small numbers, some zero).
- Sales side. Stores: three stores, each with a name, phone, email, street, city, state and zip
  code. Staffs: about ten staff members, each with first and last name, email, phone, an active
  flag, a store, and a manager who is another staff member (except the general manager).
  Customers: over a thousand customers, each with first and last name, an optional phone, an email,
  a street address, city, state and zip code, concentrated in New York, California and Texas.
- Orders: over a thousand orders across the three stores over a two-to-three-year period, each with
  a customer, an order status (pending, processing, rejected, completed; most completed), an order
  date, a required date, a shipped date (null when not shipped), the store and the staff member who
  handled it. Order items: each order has one or more lines, each for one product with a quantity
  (one or two), the list price at the time, and a discount fraction (a few common values such as
  5, 7, 10 or 20 percent).

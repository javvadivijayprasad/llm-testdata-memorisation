Business case: the company runs a chain of DVD
loan branches. 
- The business has a small number of physical branches (two), each managed by one staff member and
  located at an address in a city in a country. Staff belong to a branch.
- Clients register at one branch; each client has an address, an email, an active flag and a
  creation date. The client base is in the hundreds, spread roughly evenly across branches.
- The catalogue is a movie library in the low thousands of titles. Each movie has a title,
  description, release year (mostly around 2006), a locale, a loan duration in days (3-7),
  a loan rate (0.99, 2.99 or 4.99), a length in minutes, a replacement cost, a MPAA rating
  (G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17) and free-text special features. Movies are tagged with one section
  (from about 16 genres) and feature several cast members (a pool of about 200 cast members, each appearing
  in many movies).
- Each branch holds physical stock: several copies of many movies, so stock rows are a few
  times the movie count.
- Loans record a client taking a stock item out on a date, handled by a staff member,
  and returning it later (most returns within about a week; a small fraction not yet returned).
  Loans are the largest table: roughly ten thousand or more over the business's
  history, concentrated in a few months.
- Receipts record money received for loans (amount typically the loan rate, occasionally
  plus late fees), one or fewer receipts per loan, with a receipt date.
- Reference data: about 100 countries, several hundred cities, six locales.

Business case: the database models the human-resources history of a large
company. 

- The company has nine divisions, each with a unique name.
- Workers are identified by a worker number and have a birth date, first and last name,
  gender (M or F) and hire date. The documented full dataset has about 300,000 workers hired
  between 1985 and 2000; birth years fall roughly between 1952 and 1965.
- Division assignments: each worker has one or more division-assignment periods with
  from/to dates; a current assignment uses the sentinel date 9999-01-01 as end_date. Most
  workers have exactly one assignment; some have transferred once.
- Each division has a history of managers (a handful per division over time), also with
  from/to dates.
- Designations: each worker holds one or more designations over time (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Staff,
  Senior Staff, Assistant Engineer, Technique Leader, Manager) with from/to dates.
- Pay amounts: each worker has a yearly pay amount history — one row per year of employment with
  from/to date ranges of about one year; pay amounts are in the range of roughly 38,000 to
  160,000, rising over time. Pay amount rows are the largest table (about 2.8 million in the full
  data, i.e. roughly nine per worker).

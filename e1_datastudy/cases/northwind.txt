Business case: Northwind models a specialty-foods import/export trading company.
Source: the classic Microsoft Northwind documentation and the northwind_psql README, not the
shipped data.

- Products (dozens) belong to one of eight categories (beverages, condiments, confections,
  dairy, grains/cereals, meat/poultry, produce, seafood) and are supplied by one of a few dozen
  suppliers. Each product has a unit price, quantity-per-unit description, units in stock,
  units on order, reorder level and a discontinued flag (most are not discontinued).
- Customers are companies (on the order of one hundred), mostly in Europe and the Americas,
  with contact name/title, address, phone and optional fax. Customer demographics tables exist
  but are rarely populated.
- A small sales staff (fewer than ten employees) with names, titles, birth and hire dates, home
  addresses, and a reports-to hierarchy under one manager. Employees are assigned to sales
  territories, each in a region (four regions).
- Orders: several hundred orders over roughly two years, each placed by a customer, handled by
  an employee, shipped via one of three shippers, with order, required and shipped dates
  (shipped usually a few days after order; a few not yet shipped), freight cost and ship-to
  address. Each order has a few order-detail lines (product, unit price, quantity, discount of
  0-25%); order details are the largest table (thousands of rows).
- Reference data: a table of US states.

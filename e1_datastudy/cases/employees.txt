Business case: the "employees" sample database models the human-resources history of a large
company. Source: the MySQL Employees Sample Database documentation (dev.mysql.com/doc/employee)
and the test_db README, not the shipped data.

- The company has nine departments, each with a unique name.
- Employees are identified by an employee number and have a birth date, first and last name,
  gender (M or F) and hire date. The documented full dataset has about 300,000 employees hired
  between 1985 and 2000; birth years fall roughly between 1952 and 1965.
- Department assignments: each employee has one or more department-assignment periods with
  from/to dates; a current assignment uses the sentinel date 9999-01-01 as to_date. Most
  employees have exactly one assignment; some have transferred once.
- Each department has a history of managers (a handful per department over time), also with
  from/to dates.
- Titles: each employee holds one or more titles over time (Engineer, Senior Engineer, Staff,
  Senior Staff, Assistant Engineer, Technique Leader, Manager) with from/to dates.
- Salaries: each employee has a yearly salary history — one row per year of employment with
  from/to date ranges of about one year; salaries are in the range of roughly 38,000 to
  160,000, rising over time. Salary rows are the largest table (about 2.8 million in the full
  data, i.e. roughly nine per employee).

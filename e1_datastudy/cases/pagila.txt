Business case: Pagila (PostgreSQL port of the Sakila sample database) models a chain of DVD
rental stores. Source: pagila README and the MySQL Sakila documentation (dev.mysql.com/doc/sakila),
not the shipped data.

- The business has a small number of physical stores (two), each managed by one staff member and
  located at an address in a city in a country. Staff belong to a store.
- Customers register at one store; each customer has an address, an email, an active flag and a
  creation date. The customer base is in the hundreds, spread roughly evenly across stores.
- The catalogue is a film library in the low thousands of titles. Each film has a title,
  description, release year (mostly around 2006), a language, a rental duration in days (3-7),
  a rental rate (0.99, 2.99 or 4.99), a length in minutes, a replacement cost, an MPAA rating
  (G, PG, PG-13, R, NC-17) and free-text special features. Films are tagged with one category
  (from about 16 genres) and feature several actors (a pool of about 200 actors, each appearing
  in many films).
- Each store holds physical inventory: several copies of many films, so inventory rows are a few
  times the film count.
- Rentals record a customer taking an inventory item out on a date, handled by a staff member,
  and returning it later (most returns within about a week; a small fraction not yet returned).
  Rentals are the largest table: on the order of ten thousand or more over the business's
  history, concentrated in a few months.
- Payments record money received for rentals (amount typically the rental rate, occasionally
  plus late fees), one or fewer payments per rental, with a payment date.
- Reference data: about 100 countries, several hundred cities, six languages.

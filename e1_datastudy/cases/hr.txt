Business case: HR is the human-resources schema of a mid-sized company with offices in several
countries, used as the standard sample schema for an enterprise database.
Source: the Oracle Database Sample Schemas documentation (HR schema description), not the
shipped data.

- Regions: four world regions. Countries: about 25 countries, each in one region, identified by a
  two-letter ISO code.
- Locations: about two dozen office locations, each with a street address, postal code, city,
  state or province (optional) and a country.
- Departments: around 27 departments (administration, marketing, purchasing, human resources,
  shipping, IT, public relations, sales, executive, finance, accounting, and others), each at one
  location and most with a manager who is an employee; some departments have no employees.
- Jobs: about 19 job roles (president, administration vice president, administration assistant,
  finance manager, accountant, accounting manager, public accountant, sales manager, sales
  representative, purchasing manager, purchasing clerk, stock manager, stock clerk, shipping clerk,
  IT programmer, marketing manager, marketing representative, HR representative, public relations
  representative), each with a minimum and maximum salary.
- Employees: a little over a hundred employees, each with first and last name, a unique email
  (short, derived from the name), an optional phone number, a hire date within a few years, a job,
  a salary within the job's range, an optional commission percentage (only sales staff have one),
  a manager who is another employee (all except the president), and a department (one employee has
  none). Most employees work in shipping and sales.
- Job history: a small number of records of employees' previous jobs, each with a start date, an
  end date, the job held and the department, for employees who changed roles.
